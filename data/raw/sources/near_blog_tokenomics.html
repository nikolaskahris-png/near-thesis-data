Evolving NEAR Tokenomics

February 24, 2026

TL;DR

  • NEAR is now primed to capture value from growth and adoption: fully unlocked supply, halved inflation, live onchain governance, 5+ years on mainnet with 100% uptime, real product revenue. 
  • NEAR sits directly in the path of global liquidity and agentic commerce: Intents has already settled $13B+ in cross-chain volume and is scaling toward much larger markets.
  • NEAR Intents fee switch is live: an automatic revenue-sharing mechanism with third-party distribution channels, with all payouts denominated in $NEAR. 
  • Revenue directed to $NEAR buybacks: a governance-based structure can deploy assets for buyback and earn and other supply-management approaches. NEAR Intents has already directed revenue to 1M in $NEAR buyback.
  • NEAR is accelerating a deflationary economic flywheel through increased network usage and ecosystem growth.
  • $NEAR is the anchor for the AI economy: NEAR tokenomics are evolving in step with product architecture so economic activity is internalized at the protocol layer. 

NEAR is entering a new phase of economic maturity. After more than five years of continuous mainnet operation with 100% uptime, NEAR stands out as one of the most transparent and battle‑tested cryptoeconomic systems in the industry. The network’s token supply is now fully unlocked. The halving upgrade has reduced maximum annual inflation by 50%. Onchain governance through House of Stake is live and actively passing proposals. Core products, such as NEAR Intents and NEAR DCML, are generating real, recurring revenue.

NEAR has evolved well beyond a conventional Layer‑1 blockchain. Today, NEAR operates as a unified commerce layer for assets and agents: settling cross‑chain trades, powering AI‑native applications, and providing cryptographic solutions around execution and privacy at scale.

The focus now for NEAR is productization and economic refinement: strengthening how value flows through the system, how incentives are aligned, and how the $NEAR token remains structurally central as usage scales across NEAR Intents and NEAR DCML.

This post outlines how NEAR tokenomics are evolving to:

  • Grow core product revenue
  • Capture value at the base layer
  • Accelerate a deflationary economic flywheel through increased network usage and ecosystem growth
  • Increase transparency and governance over economic mechanisms
  • Position $NEAR as the asset for the next era of onchain and agentic commerce

The goal of this post is to provide a rigorous understanding of NEAR’s economic system as it exists today, and how it is being designed to translate usage and revenue into durable economic effects.

Scaling Value Capture in the NEAR Ecosystem

NEAR’s economic model is increasingly driven by product‑level revenues that flow back to the protocol. Two products sit at the core of this shift: NEAR Intents and NEAR DCML, both underpinned by NEAR Protocol.

Unlike cryptoeconomic models that rely primarily on external applications for value capture, NEAR is intentionally building native products that capture key economic activity at the protocol layer. As Bitwise explains in a NEAR overview report, NEAR “internalizes the economics of certain activities in a way that benefits the base protocol.”

NEAR Intents: Settlement for Global Onchain Liquidity

NEAR Intents is the universal liquidity protocol that allows users and agents to express outcomes rather than manage execution paths. Instead of navigating bridges, routes, and fragmented liquidity, users simply specify their intent. The system handles the how.

While NEAR Intents has already powered over $13 billion in cross‑chain volume across major assets and chains, it has a significantly larger addressable market, positioning NEAR Intents as settlement infrastructure directly in the flow of global crypto liquidity. This includes the Stablecoin Transport Protocol that is currently being developed on top of the NEAR Intents architecture.

  • Centralized exchanges process approximately $26-37B in daily spot volume (The Block).
  • Decentralized exchanges process roughly $10B daily (Coingecko).
  • Stablecoin supply is projected to grow to $2T by 2028, with increasing demand for large, efficient cross‑chain transfers (McKinsey). 

NEAR Intents is already operating as core settlement infrastructure for onchain markets and tokenized assets, facilitating cross-chain DeFi execution and emerging institutional workflows. As stablecoin transport and tokenized asset settlement continue to scale across onchain and institutional markets, NEAR Intents sits in the execution path of a materially larger share of global transactional volume.

NEAR Intents Revenue Model and Distribution: The Switch

The economics of NEAR Intents is designed around scale and distribution. Fees are composed of:

  • Protocol fees, which flow directly to NEAR Protocol
  • Distribution fees, which are shared between NEAR and integration partners

Protocol fees are intentionally low to support broad adoption. Distribution fees, by contrast, represent a growing revenue lever as NEAR expands its footprint across wallets, dApps, financial platforms, and agentic systems.

NEAR Intents has already introduced an automatic revenue-sharing mechanism with third-party distribution channels. This mechanism establishes a standardized model for how Intents usage translates into recurring, protocol-native revenue. Additionally, all revenue-share payouts will be denominated in $NEAR, reinforcing the $NEAR token as the unit of account for Intents-generated fees and directly linking broader distribution to token-native demand. 

Another near‑term revenue driver includes expanding native front‑end usage for higher direct value capture. Together, revenue sharing and native front-end usage ensure a model where growth in Intents usage directly increases protocol‑level economic activity without having to increase costs for end users.

NEAR DCML: Infrastructure for the Agentic Economy

NEAR DCML extends NEAR’s economic reach far beyond traditional blockchain use cases into confidential, verifiable AI infrastructure designed for agent-native markets. 

The NEAR team has been building at the intersection of AI and cryptography since 2017. The team has published foundational research—including Decentralized Confidential Machine Learning and Proof of Response—exploring how AI systems can operate securely, verifiably, and autonomously in decentralized environments. Building on this research, NEAR AI is now shipping production-grade infrastructure and integrations for both enterprises and end users:

  • NEAR AI Cloud: hardware‑backed private inference via TEEs
  • NEAR AI Private Chat: privacy‑preserving AI interactions for everyday use
  • NEAR AI Agent Market: decentralized marketplace for AI agents to transact with full economic autonomy, integrated with NEAR Intents
  • OpenClaw on NEAR AI Cloud: always-on agent with cryptographic privacy guarantees

NEAR AI is actively serving enterprises and end users through integrations with major platforms including Brave’s widely-adopted privacy browser, the robotics OS OpenMind, and confidential cloud provider Phala.

NEAR AI Economic Design and Market Context

NEAR DCML is explicitly designed to enable usage of native $NEAR, ensuring that AI activity is directly linked to the protocol’s economic layer:

  • Compute and inference services are payable in $NEAR
  • AI agents are payable in $NEAR to operate and maintain state
  • Agent transactions settle on NEAR or via NEAR Intents, generating fees denominated in $NEAR 

As agentic systems become embedded in commerce, finance, and enterprise workflows, NEAR AI’s infrastructure is designed to support recurring, usage-driven demand for inference, memory, GPU utilization, and other AI capabilities. Because these services are natively denominated in $NEAR, increased agent activity translates directly into higher protocol-level economic throughput.

Industry research highlights the scale of this opportunity. Morgan Stanley estimates that agentic shoppers in e-commerce alone could represent $385 billion in annual volume by 2030, while IDC projects that AI will contribute approximately $19.9 trillion to the global economy. If NEAR AI captures even a modest share of these emerging workloads, it would imply processing significant volumes of economic activity in a future dominated by agentic commerce.

Confidential and verifiable AI is a foundational requirement for this emerging economy. Enterprises and governments require privacy-preserving infrastructure to deploy sensitive workloads, while end users increasingly demand control over how their data and AI systems operate. NEAR AI is uniquely positioned to meet these requirements by combining hardware-enforced security, verifiable privacy, sharded scalability, and a production track record with over five years of continuous mainnet uptime.

As NEAR AI’s product suite continues to expand, $NEAR-native payments will remain central to how AI-driven activity settles and accrues economic value within the NEAR ecosystem. As Bitwise clearly explains, “In a world where agents transact, NEAR settles.”

Aligning Revenue With Long-Term Ecosystem Growth

Generating core product revenue is only one dimension of a sustainable cryptoeconomic model. The other is translating that revenue into durable effects for token dynamics. 

Revenue from NEAR products, including Intents, is transparent and visible onchain. A governance-based treasury structure is being established to manage these product revenues in the interest of long-term ecosystem growth. This design will seek to direct all revenues from NEAR products—Intents, AI, and future offerings—into this structure. This governance structure would retain control of the revenue, with the ability to redeploy assets for ecosystem expansion.

This governance framework provides the NEAR ecosystem with a range of economic tools that may be deployed at the discretion of governance participants, subject to applicable legal and regulatory requirements. These tools include ad hoc buybacks of $NEAR, staking or locking of acquired tokens as part of a buyback and earn strategy, and other supply-management approaches. No specific mechanism is guaranteed or committed to, and governance retains full discretion over whether, when, and how any such tools are applied.

The Intents fee switch and denomination in $NEAR—now live—is an active step toward translating product revenue into value accrual. NEAR Intents has already directed revenue to over 1M in $NEAR buyback.

NEAR’s governance framework allows for a blend of economic tools, adjusted over time based on network conditions. As network usage increases, NEAR can reach a threshold where tokens removed from circulation exceed new issuance, driving NEAR tokenomics into a deflationary flywheel. 

To improve transparency, NEAR is introducing a NEAR Revenue Dashboard. This indicator gives a clear, ecosystem-wide reference for understanding net supply dynamics, improving transparency around how protocol economics evolve alongside network usage.

Conclusion: Toward a Unified Economic Stack

NEAR’s tokenomics are evolving in step with its product architecture. As NEAR Intents and NEAR AI expand NEAR’s role in cross-chain markets and agent-driven workflows, economic activity is increasingly internalized at the protocol layer to support direct, transparent value accrual to NEAR through fees, settlement, and governance-controlled mechanisms.

The long-term direction is a vertically integrated, non-custodial economic stack that combines cross-chain execution, confidential computation, and agent-native financial workflows, all within an intuitive user experience. In this system, products are not adjacent to the protocol—they are economic extensions of it.

At the center of this unified architecture is $NEAR, which functions simultaneously as the settlement asset, the unit of account for protocol services, and the coordination mechanism for governance and security. As usage scales, $NEAR's role as the settlement asset, fee denomination, and governance token is reinforced through transparent, governance-approved economic processes and ensures $NEAR is the anchor for a user‑owned internet and AI economy.

Rather than optimizing for short-term incentives, NEAR is formalizing an economic model in which usage-driven growth, governance, and supply dynamics reinforce one another over time. This holistic approach helps ensure NEAR is positioned to operate as essential and durable infrastructure for onchain markets and the fast-emerging agentic economy.

Appendix: Definitions and Terminology

This appendix provides non-exhaustive definitions intended to clarify terminology used throughout this post. These definitions are descriptive and provided for informational purposes only.

Tokenomics: The economic design of a blockchain network, including token supply, issuance, utility, incentive structures, governance mechanisms, and how economic value flows through the system.

Value Capture: The mechanisms by which economic activity occurring within an ecosystem generates revenue or fees that are directly collected by the protocol, its products, or governance-controlled entities. Value capture refers to where and how economic value is collected (e.g., protocol fees, distribution fees, service payments).

Emissions: The issuance of new $NEAR according to protocol-defined rules, primarily used to incentivize validators and secure the network.

Effective Emissions: A metric representing the net change in liquid $NEAR supply after accounting for protocol emissions minus tokens removed from liquid circulation through mechanisms such as buybacks, burning, staking, or long-term locking. 

Circulating Supply: The portion of the total $NEAR supply that is liquid and available for transfer on the open market, excluding tokens that are locked, staked, vested, or otherwise restricted.

Buy Back and Earn: An economic mechanism in which protocol or ecosystem revenues are used to purchase $NEAR that are then staked, locked, or otherwise removed from liquid circulation while remaining part of the total supply.

Treasury Structure: A governance-controlled entity or set of smart contracts responsible for receiving ecosystem revenues, managing accumulated $NEAR, and executing approved economic actions in a transparent and rules-based manner.

Protocol Fees: Fees generated by NEAR-native systems that flow directly to NEAR Protocol or governance-controlled Tr structures.

Distribution Fees: Fees generated through third-party integrations and distribution channels, shared between NEAR Protocol and ecosystem partners to incentivize adoption and usage.
